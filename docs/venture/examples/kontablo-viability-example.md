# Kontablo Viability Example

> **Example Application of the Business Viability Framework**
> 
> This document demonstrates how to apply the Business Viability Framework to the **venture-instance-kontablo** venture. It serves as a reference for applying the framework to accounting ontology and reconciliation engine business models.

---

## Overview

**Venture:** venture-instance-kontablo (Kontablo - Accounting Esperanto Engine)

**Product:** A graph-based, UUID-keyed universal accounting ontology that bridges:
- Local jurisdictional charts
- International standards (IFRS/XBRL)
- The agentic economy (MCP/A2A/AP2)
- Blockchain/DeFi protocols

**Target Market:** Multi-jurisdictional businesses, accounting firms, fintech companies, DeFi protocols

**Value Proposition:** Unified accounting ontology that eliminates reconciliation complexity across jurisdictions and standards.

---

## Step 1: Define the Minimum Viable Client (MVC)

### MVC Definition

| Dimension | Criteria | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Location** | Global (multi-jurisdictional) | Kontablo is jurisdiction-agnostic by design |
| **Size** | ≥10 employees AND ≥USD 500K annual revenue | Capacity to afford enterprise solutions |
| **Industry/Type** | Accounting firms, fintech companies, DeFi protocols | Primary users of accounting systems |
| **Pain Point** | Jurisdictional chart reconciliation, multi-standard compliance (IFRS/XBRL), agentic economy integration | Core problem Kontablo solves |
| **Exclusion** | Already using enterprise accounting software with multi-jurisdictional support (SAP, Oracle, Workday) | No need for standalone ontology |
| **Payment Capacity** | Established business, budget for compliance tools, access to payment methods | Ability to pay enterprise pricing |

**MVC Statement:**
> A multi-jurisdictional accounting firm, fintech company, or DeFi protocol with ≥10 employees and ≥USD 500K annual revenue, struggling with chart of accounts reconciliation between local standards and IFRS/XBRL, and lacking integration with the agentic economy (MCP/A2A/AP2). Excluded: firms already using enterprise-grade accounting systems with multi-jurisdictional support.

## Step 4: Competitive Landscape and Pricing

### Competitive Mapping

| Competitor | Type | Offering | Price | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Manual/Excel** | Substitute | Spreadsheets, custom scripts | "Free" (sunk cost) | Familiar, flexible | Error-prone, no auditability, no automation |
| **SAP** | Direct | Enterprise accounting | USD 50K–500K/year | Comprehensive, established | Expensive, complex, vendor lock-in |
| **Oracle** | Direct | Enterprise accounting | USD 100K–1M+/year | Global, feature-rich | Very expensive, complex |
| **Workday** | Direct | Cloud accounting | USD 30K–200K/year | Modern, cloud-native | Limited jurisdictional support |
| **Xero** | Indirect | SMB accounting | USD 10–100/month | Affordable, easy to use | No multi-jurisdictional, no enterprise features |
| **QuickBooks** | Indirect | SMB accounting | USD 20–200/month | Popular, user-friendly | Limited to single jurisdiction |
| **Custom Solutions** | Indirect | In-house systems | Varies | Tailored to needs | Expensive to build/maintain |

**Kontablo Positioning:**
- **Type:** Enabler/Platform
- **Offering:** Universal accounting ontology + reconciliation engine
- **Integration:** Works with existing systems (SAP, Oracle, etc.)
- **Differentiator:** Jurisdiction-agnostic, standard-agnostic, agentic-economy ready

### Pricing Strategy

**Anchoring:**
- Enterprise accounting software: USD 30K–500K/year
- Compliance consulting: USD 50K–200K/year
- Data reconciliation tools: USD 10K–50K/year

**Kontablo Pricing:**

| Tier | Target | Price | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | Small accounting firms | USD 1,000/month | Single jurisdiction, basic reconciliation |
| **Professional** | Mid-size firms | USD 5,000/month | Multi-jurisdiction, IFRS/XBRL support |
| **Enterprise** | Large firms, fintech | USD 15,000/month | Full ontology, agentic economy integration |
| **Custom** | DeFi protocols, global enterprises | Custom | Tailored solutions, premium support |

**Anchoring Justification:**
- Positioned at **3–10%** of enterprise accounting software cost
- Justified by **time savings** (estimated 50–80% reduction in reconciliation time)
- **ROI:** For a firm spending USD 100K/year on reconciliation, Kontablo at USD 15K/month = 180K/year saves 82K/year (45% net savings)

**Value-Based Pricing:**
- **Economic Value:** Estimated 50–80% reduction in reconciliation time
- **Time Savings:** 100 hours/month at USD 100/hour = USD 10K/month
- **Price Capture:** 15–30% of value = USD 1.5K–3K/month
- **Actual Price:** USD 5K–15K/month (captures 50–150% of time savings)

## Step 5: Unit Economics

### Per-Customer Metrics (Enterprise Tier)

| Metric | Value | Calculation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ticket** | USD 15,000/month | | |
| **COGS** | USD 2,000/month | Infrastructure: USD 500, Support: USD 1,000, Success: USD 500 | |
| **Gross Margin** | USD 13,000/month | Ticket - COGS | 86.7% |
| **CAC** | USD 50,000 | Sales: USD 30K, Marketing: USD 10K, Onboarding: USD 10K | Enterprise sales |
| **Churn** | 2% monthly | 24% annually | Enterprise retention |
| **LTV** | USD 650,000 | Ticket × Gross Margin % × (1/Churn) = 15K × 86.7% × (1/0.02) | |

### Key Ratios

| Ratio | Value | Target | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LTV:CAC** | 13:1 | > 3:1 | ✅ Excellent |
| **Payback Period** | 3.8 months | < 12 months | ✅ Excellent |
| **Gross Margin %** | 86.7% | > 60% | ✅ Excellent |

## Step 6: Break-Even and Fragility Test

### Fixed Costs

| Category | Monthly Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Development | USD 20,000 | Engineering team |
| Operations | USD 5,000 | Infrastructure, tools |
| Sales & Marketing | USD 15,000 | Enterprise sales team |
| G&A | USD 10,000 | Legal, accounting, admin |
| **Total** | **USD 50,000/month** | |

### Break-Even Calculation

```
Break-Even (clients) = Fixed Costs / Gross Margin per Client
                      = 50,000 / 13,000
                      = 3.85 → 4 clients (rounded up)
```

**Break-Even = 4 clients**

### Fragility Test

| Metric | Value | Ratio | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Break-Even | 4 clients | | |
| SOM Optimista (Year 1) | 6,000 clients | 4 / 6,000 = **0.067%** | ✅ STRONG |
| SOM Realista (Year 1) | 4,500 clients | 4 / 4,500 = **0.089%** | ✅ STRONG |

**Fragility Ratio: 0.067–0.089% → STRONG** ✅

**Interpretation:**
- Break-even requires only 4 clients
- SOM Year 1 is 3,000–6,000 clients
- Model is **structurally sound** with huge margin for error
- Even with conservative assumptions, model easily clears break-even

## Conclusion

### Summary

The **venture-instance-kontablo** business model passes all viability tests with excellent margins:

- **TAM:** 200K–400K potential customers
- **SAM:** 40K–80K qualified customers
- **SOM Year 1:** 3K–6K reachable customers
- **Break-Even:** 4 clients
- **Fragility Ratio:** 0.07% → **STRONG**
- **Unit Economics:** LTV:CAC = 13:1, Margin = 86.7%, Payback = 3.8 months
- **Scorecard:** 8.8/10 → **PROCEED**

### Decision

**✅ GO** — Proceed with development and market entry.

### Critical Next Steps

1. **Validate Demand** (Priority #1): Secure 5+ LOIs and 1+ paid pilot
2. **Build Sales Capability**: Hire enterprise sales team
3. **Develop Channel Partnerships**: Accounting firms, consultants, system integrators
4. **Execute Pilot Program**: Prove value with early adopters
5. **Scale Based on Validation**: Expand based on pilot results

### Key Insight

The **break-even point (4 clients)** is so low relative to the **SOM (3K–6K)** that the model is essentially **de-risked** from a market size perspective. The primary risk is **execution** (sales, delivery, validation), not market viability.
